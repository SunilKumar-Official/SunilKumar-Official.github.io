AboutSunil Kumar

I didn’t start in data. I started with the problems behind it.

My career began on the phone and in the chat window, working directly with customers in customer service and retention. The job was to understand why customers were unhappy, identify what they actually needed, and find a solution before they decided to leave.

That experience shaped how I approach data today. It taught me to look beyond the numbers, question what they represent, and understand the real-world decisions they are supposed to support.

Workforce management was my bridge into analytics. Forecasting demand, assessing capacity, and explaining the gap between expected workload and available resources turned data into something tangible: decisions that affected teams, customers, and business performance every day.

From there, I deliberately moved deeper into the technical side of data, developing my skills in SQL, Python, and data engineering. I wanted to do more than interpret reports. I wanted to understand how the data behind them was collected, transformed, validated, and delivered — and what it would take to make that process reliable.

Because a report is only as trustworthy as the data pipeline behind it.

That principle now shapes the work I do: establishing consistent KPI definitions across teams, designing and administering the data platform that supports our analytics, and owning production data pipelines end to end. It also means investigating the less visible problems — missing records, inconsistent definitions, and silent data gaps — that can undermine a report long before anyone questions its accuracy.

Today, I bring together operational understanding, business analysis, analytics, and data engineering to build solutions that go beyond reporting what happened. My focus is on making data reliable, insights meaningful, and decisions better informed.

Where I’m headed
01

Clean data, at the source

Large-scale data management: making sure data is clean and trustworthy as close to its source as possible, not patched up after the fact — the same instinct behind standardizing a fragmented KPI methodology, finding a pipeline’s silent coverage gaps before they became someone else’s problem, and building validation that catches a bad number before it reaches a report.

02

Language models, with care

Using language models to help interpret the parts of a conversation or a dataset that simple rules can’t reliably judge, without pretending a model’s confidence is the same thing as its accuracy. I built and rigorously evaluated exactly that — comparing models not just on accuracy, but on whether they’d give you the same answer twice.
